One account fee at onboarding, then a standardized trading fee across every asset class. Nothing recurring.

## Account fee

Paid once, based on your account type, configuration, and any add-ons. There is no recurring evaluation fee.

## Trading fees

A single open-and-close model, applied evenly across asset classes.

| Asset class     | Opening fee | Closing fee | Round trip |
| --------------- | ----------- | ----------- | ---------- |
| **Stocks**      | 0.010%      | 0.010%      | 0.020%     |
| **ETFs**        | 0.010%      | 0.010%      | 0.020%     |
| **Commodities** | 0.006%      | 0.006%      | 0.012%     |
| **Indices**     | 0.007%      | 0.007%      | 0.014%     |
| **Forex**       | 0.005%      | 0.005%      | 0.010%     |
| **Crypto**      | 0.020%      | 0.020%      | 0.040%     |

The venues price execution differently beneath the surface. DojiFunded standardizes that into one trader-facing schedule, so execution stays transparent, strategy analysis stays clean, and costs stay comparable across assets.

## Venue costs that still vary

Some costs are set by the venue, not the platform, and move with the asset and the market. GMX can add funding fees; Ostium can add rollover fees. Both are shown in the platform where they apply.

## Before trading, confirm

* The account price at checkout
* The trading fee for your asset class
* Any venue funding or rollover cost shown in the platform
* How those costs land in your PnL and history

## How execution and routing work

<Note>
  This is the most important point on the page. DojiFunded does not mirror every order on-chain one to one. Your activity is recorded first in the DojiFunded **internal ledger and risk engine**, and the net exposure across traders is then aggregated and routed on-chain to venues such as Ostium. As volume scales, DojiFunded operates as a funded trading and risk orchestration layer that sources execution liquidity from its partners. Your PnL is tracked identically whichever way a trade is handled. We state this plainly here, and again in the rulebook, so you always know exactly what your fills represent.
</Note>
